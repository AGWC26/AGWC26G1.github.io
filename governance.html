Leadership • Accountability • Stewardship

Governance designed to protect the mission and serve the community.

Angel Grips Wellness Centre is guided by a formal governance structure intended to support responsible leadership, transparent decision-making, financial accountability and preservation of the Centre's founding vision.

The Centre is currently seeking formal NGO registration in Trinidad and Tobago.

Governance overview

A structured framework for continuity, accountability and service.

AGWC operates under a written Constitution and is overseen through a Founders Council, a Board of Directors, Executive Officers and specialised committees. Together, these bodies provide strategic oversight, protect organisational assets, support programme delivery and preserve the values associated with Errol Alphege Grant.

The governance framework is intended to balance institutional continuity with responsible participation by members, volunteers, technical advisers and community partners.

Governance structure

How leadership and oversight are organised.

Each governance body has a distinct role while remaining accountable to the Centre's mission, Constitution and approved policies.

01

Founders Council

The Founders Council consists of the five Founding Members and serves as custodian of AGWC's founding vision, institutional memory, protected constitutional provisions and long-term mission.

02

Board of Directors

The Board comprises seven Directors, including five Founding Directors and up to two additional Directors. It provides strategic leadership and oversight of programmes, finances, policies, assets and organisational performance.

03

Executive Officers

Executive Officers support the administration of the Centre and include the President, Vice President, Secretary, Treasurer, Public Relations Officer, Programme Coordinator and Director-at-Large.

04

Standing Committees

Specialised committees support finance and fundraising, programme and wellness services, animal welfare and facilities, membership and volunteers, environmental sustainability and horticulture, and equestrian standards and training.

Accountability and financial stewardship

Responsible use of funds, assets and authority.

AGWC's governance framework includes financial reporting, authorised signatories, expenditure approval procedures, conflict-of-interest requirements, annual financial review and Board responsibility for safeguarding organisational assets.

Directors and officers are expected to act honestly, in good faith and in the best interests of the Centre. Organisational income and surplus funds are to be used solely to advance AGWC's mission and approved activities.

Membership and participation

A membership organisation with defined responsibilities.

Membership is open to individuals aged eighteen years and over who support AGWC's mission, objectives and values. Membership categories include Founding, Ordinary, Volunteer, Honorary and Lifetime Members.

Participation in governance

Voting and eligibility for office are determined by membership category and good-standing requirements. Volunteer and honorary participation remain important to programme delivery even where voting rights do not apply.

Governance documents

Formal documents are maintained for regulatory, funding and organisational purposes.

AGWC does not currently publish its full Constitution on the public website. Governing documents may be provided to regulators, grant-making institutions, authorised partners and other appropriate parties upon request.
